Water science researchers rely on datasets from federal agencies, but few have been trained to access them using recommended and sustainable patterns. This course builds the data literacy to do that programmatically, and to understand what each source is best at in water research. The pilot is Python-based and was developed with the recommendations of the data providers themselves, with support from NASA, NOAA and USGS.

## What the course covers

The pilot works with three datasets:

- NASA [Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT)](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/swot/)
- NOAA [National Water Model (NWM)](https://water.noaa.gov/about/nwm)
- USGS [Water Data for the Nation (WDFN)](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/)

It has four modules:

- Module 1: Data Best Practices in Water Research
- Module 2: Federal Water Data Landscape
- Module 3: Federal Water Data Access and Retrieval
- Module 4: Federal Water Data Synthesis

By the end, participants should be able to:

- explain open science, FAIR principles and reproducibility best practices, and their benefits to science products and the science community
- outline the key elements of a data management plan and the benefits of publishing scientific data in an appropriate repository
- characterize the river data products from NASA SWOT, NOAA NWM and USGS WDFN, including relevant variables, derivation, spatial and temporal resolution, and known limitations
- adapt provided Python scripts to retrieve data from NASA SWOT, NOAA NWM and USGS WDFN using their recommended APIs and libraries
- select the most appropriate programmatic approach for downloading data at large temporal and spatial scales for different hydrologic applications
- compare and describe the capabilities and limitations of NASA SWOT, NOAA NWM and USGS WDFN data for a single flood event, at the appropriate scales and resolutions

The curriculum is developed in the open in CUAHSI's [federal-water-data-curriculum repository](https://github.com/CUAHSI/federal-water-data-curriculum) on GitHub.

## Time commitment and certificate

Expect about 20 hours over the four weeks: 14 hours of live (synchronous) sessions plus work outside class hours. Office hours are included, with opportunities to ask questions and get feedback. To earn the completion certificate, participants must pass assessments with hard deadlines: short "check your understanding" quizzes and a final presentation.

## Who it is for

The course is designed for graduate students, postdocs, early-career researchers and professionals who use federal water data in their research. Participants should have intermediate Python experience: comfortable with variables, loops and functions, able to adapt example code to install and use libraries, load data into common structures, clean and plot data, write short scripts, organize code in a Jupyter notebook and troubleshoot common errors. If those skills are new, CUAHSI recommends building them before enrolling.
