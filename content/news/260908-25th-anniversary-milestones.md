Twenty-five years ago, a group of researchers came together around a shared challenge: hydrologic science needed standing as a discipline of its own, and it needed shared infrastructure that no single university could build alone. CUAHSI was incorporated in 2001 to take that on and has evolved with the water science community, the funding landscape and advances in technology. This timeline marks milestones from the founding to the programs and data services that now serve researchers across the country.

## The milestones, in order

- **2001, incorporation.** After a community meeting at NSF, the hydrology community organized a consortium to address shared infrastructure and research needs. CUAHSI was incorporated in Washington, D.C. on June 25, 2001, with 33 founding universities.
- **2003, Cyberseminar program.** A free, open venue for water scientists to share research without travel; recordings made the content available any time. The archive now holds more than 200 presentations.
- **2008, Community Hydrologic Modeling Platform (CHyMP).** The first in a series of workshops on a community-level hydrologic modeling effort.
- **2012, HydroShare development begins.** Led by Utah State University, the Renaissance Computing Institute (RENCI) and CUAHSI, as an NSF-funded cyberinfrastructure project. It launched for community use in 2015, and operations moved to CUAHSI in 2018.
- **2015, Water Prediction Innovators Summer Institute launches,** with the University of Alabama and the NOAA National Water Center.
- **2017, CUAHSI Virtual University founded,** and CUAHSI launched JupyterHub, a free cloud computing service for water science.
- **2022, CUAHSI joins NOAA's Cooperative Institute for Research to Operations in Hydrology (CIROH)** as one of 28 institutional partners.
- **2024, HydroShare moves to the cloud.**
- **2025, the next chapter:** a new NSF Cooperative Agreement (2026 to 2028) to sustain core programs and services, alongside five new awards from CIROH.
