This month's community guest spotlight features **Amirhossein Montazeri**, a PhD student in computer science at Boise State University. He took part in the 2026 Water Prediction Innovators Summer Institute, the Data Publishing Program and WaterSoftHack.

His doctoral research uses geospatial data, remote sensing, machine learning and environmental modeling to study wildfire progression and landscape impacts. He writes that each program contributed something different: the Summer Institute gave him close work in an interdisciplinary team on hydrologic research and modeling, and working with mentors from other fields introduced him to new approaches. Across the programs he learned to work with people from hydrology, environmental science, computer science, modeling and data analytics, to communicate with researchers outside his discipline and to present to varied audiences.

He says the experience helped him appreciate the physical processes behind hydrologic problems and the importance of combining domain knowledge with data-driven methods. He hopes to keep bringing together hydrology, geospatial science, artificial intelligence, environmental modeling and reproducible data practices.
