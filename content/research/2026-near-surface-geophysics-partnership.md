Near-surface geophysics is an area where CUAHSI's community overlaps with several other NSF-funded facilities and programs. In March 2026 CEO Jordan Read met with leadership from EarthScope, the National Hazards Engineering Research Infrastructure (NHERI), the Center for Transformative Environmental Monitoring Programs (CTEMPS) and OpenTopography at a strategic partnership meeting (March 9 to 11, Seattle). The conversations identified concrete directions for supporting that shared community and for more coordination among the facilities.

That meeting led to the [2026 Near-Surface Geophysics Community Input Workshop](https://ngf.earthscope.org/events/2026-near-surface-geophysics-community-input-workshop), held August 25 and 26, 2026 in Albuquerque, New Mexico. It was sponsored by the NSF National Geophysical Facility, operated by EarthScope, and planned jointly by that facility, CTEMPS, CUAHSI, NHERI, OpenTopography and the AGU Near-surface Geophysics Section. Ninety-four attendees, mostly from the near-surface geophysics community, took part. Read and Community Relations and Training Coordinator Julia Masterman represented CUAHSI.

The workshop brought researchers, educators and the National Geophysical Facility together to assess gaps and coordination opportunities across near-surface geophysics research and education. Discussions pointed to practical coordination between the organizations, such as referring community members to each other's training and educational resources.

The five organizations (CTEMPS, NHERI, CUAHSI, OpenTopography and the NSF National Geophysical Facility) have since entered a partnership to centralize support for near-surface geophysics, which until now has had no unified center. The [workshop report](/documents/news/2026-near-surface-geophysics-workshop-report.pdf) turns the community input into a set of prioritized capabilities for the partners. In the near term they will set up the partnership's virtual center and its governance, and an online landing page for sharing resources; after that they plan to sponsor more community events, acquire instruments with current funds and new proposals, develop data tools and best practices, build a range of training programs, and create ways for people across disciplines to network and collaborate. (Update, November 12, 2026, from the [November 2026 e-newsletter](/community/newsletter/2026-november).)

Earlier in the year CUAHSI and the EarthScope-operated National Geophysical Facility ran the joint [Integrating Hydrology and Geophysics webinar series](/about/impact/hydrology-geophysics-and-neon-series-2026).
