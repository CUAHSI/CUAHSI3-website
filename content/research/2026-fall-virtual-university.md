The Fall 2026 [CUAHSI Virtual University](/learn-train/programs/virtual-university) (CVU) runs September 8 to December 3, 2026. It is a semester-long, multi-institution graduate course made of four-week modules on specialized hydrology topics. Instructors teach their modules as part of their regular duties, and students can take up to three modules for credit at their home institutions.

Instructors and modules were solicited in the first quarter and selected with the Education and Outreach Advisory Committee. Eight instructors from eight institutions teach this year:

- **Emily Burt**, University of Nevada, Reno: Stable Isotopes in Hydrology
- **Laura Condon**, University of Arizona: Integrated Hydrologic Modeling with ParFlow
- **Erkan Istanbulluoglu**, University of Washington: Python-Based Watershed Modeling Using Landlab
- **Meetpal Kukal**, University of Idaho: Standardized ET Modeling for Agricultural Applications
- **Steven Loheide**, University of Wisconsin-Madison: Ecohydrology of Groundwater Dependent Ecosystems
- **Zach Perzan**, University of Nevada, Las Vegas: Reactive Transport Modeling in Hydrologic Systems
- **Jan Seibert**, University of Zurich: Hydrological Catchment Modeling Using Bucket-Type Models
- **David Tarboton**, Utah State University: Applying GIS for Terrain and Watershed Analysis in Hydrology

This year every module was asked to include a data competency component. Seven of the eight did, and CUAHSI staff will provide supplemental training for the remaining module. Community Relations and Training Coordinator Julia Masterman onboarded the instructors and introduced them to CUAHSI's other tools (HydroShare, the open learning platform and HydroLearn). At the time of the report, 48 students across the eight institutions were enrolled; final numbers will be in the next report. CUAHSI plans a joint instructor recruitment event with HydroLearn at the AGU Fall Meeting. The program is supported by [NSF award 2535162](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/show-award/?AWD_ID=2535162), "Sustained Resources: Advancing Water Science Through Integrated Water Data Management and Community Support" (January 2026 to December 2028). See also last year's [74-student term](/about/impact/virtual-university-fall-2025).
