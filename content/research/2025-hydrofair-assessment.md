CUAHSI and DataONE completed a comprehensive FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) assessment of HydroShare, the first evidence-based quantification of metadata quality across the platform's entire public and published dataset catalog.

The assessment used the MetaDig framework, which applies 52 evaluation criteria to each dataset's metadata. DataONE extended MetaDig to ingest metadata expressed in the schema.org vocabulary that HydroShare uses, making it possible to assess HydroShare datasets through the same lens applied to other major earth science repositories.

Key findings: most public resources (publicly available but not yet formally published with a DOI) pass approximately 25–40% of the evaluated FAIR criteria. Published resources — those formally published with a permanent DOI — score higher, passing 38–48% of criteria. As expected, HydroShare scores well in the Findable and Accessible categories, where its metadata is more complete, and lower in Interoperable and Reusable, where the assessment checks for metadata elements such as attribute precision and measurement scale that are not currently captured in HydroShare's metadata profile.

This assessment establishes a quantitative baseline that CUAHSI can use to track improvement over time as metadata quality initiatives — including the Data Publishing Cohort program and future metadata schema enhancements — take effect. The full methodology and results will inform HydroShare's metadata profile roadmap under the new cooperative agreement (EAR-2535162).

The assessment was carried out with [DataONE](https://www.dataone.org/) against the [FAIR principles](https://www.go-fair.org/fair-principles/) on [HydroShare](https://www.hydroshare.org/). Covered in the [CUAHSI 2025 Annual Report](/documents/annual-reports/annual-report-2025.pdf).
