CUAHSI awarded eight HydroInformatics Innovation Fellowships (HIF) in 2025, selected by a review panel of Informatics Advisory Committee members and prior HIF recipients from 28 applications submitted by researchers at 23 universities. The 2025 awards totaled $39,800.

The 2025 awardees and their projects:

**Adnan Dehghaninahrkhalaji** (Rice University) — *Continental-scale river streamflow database for benchmarking, calibrating, and training physical and AI models.* Building a harmonized, community-accessible streamflow dataset designed specifically for training and benchmarking hydrologic AI models at continental scale.

**Omowumi Erukubami** (University of Delaware) — *Framework for evaluating groundwater and storm and sanitary sewer interactions.* Developing tools to assess coupled groundwater–sewer dynamics, an interaction increasingly relevant to urban flood and water quality management.

**Annette Hilton** (UC Santa Barbara) — *A century of California water data restored by machine learning.* Using machine learning to digitize and rescue historical water records that exist only as paper archives, restoring a century of California hydrologic data for modern use.

**Keira Johnson** (Oregon State University) — *HydroViz: Building capacity for environmental data literacy and watershed synthesis.* An open-source hydroinformatics platform for undergraduate education, combining data literacy training with watershed-scale synthesis tools.

**Caitlin Mayernik** (Oregon State University) — *Boosting synthesis of local soil moisture datasets for an AI-ready global database.* Harmonizing local soil moisture observations into a globally consistent database to support large-scale analysis and AI model development.

**Jeil Oh** (University of Texas at Austin) — *Scalable, adaptive, risk-informed hydrological network design.* An open-source toolkit for the hydrologic community to optimize sensor network design under uncertainty and risk constraints.

**Seongeun Park** (Carnegie Mellon University) — *Rescuing water infrastructure operational knowledge via AI-driven data integration.* Applying AI to integrate and rescue operational knowledge from water infrastructure systems, preserving institutional knowledge that is at risk of being lost.

**Alex Saunders** (University of Arizona) — *A community dataset and deep learning toolkit for remotely sensed historical flood exposure in the US Midwest.* Building a community flood exposure dataset and deep learning tools for historical flood detection from remote sensing archives.

About the fellowship: the [HydroInformatics Innovation Fellowship page](https://www.cuahsi.org/events/hydroinformatics-innovation-fellowship). The [CUAHSI 2025 Annual Report](/documents/annual-reports/annual-report-2025.pdf) lists the eight awardees.
