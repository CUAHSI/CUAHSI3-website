## CUAHSI celebrates 25 years: A challenge for today's hydrologic scientists

As part of CUAHSI's 25th anniversary, this issue opens a series of reflections from people who helped shape CUAHSI's story. The first invited contributor is **Rick Hooper**, who served as CUAHSI's Executive Director from 2002 to 2017. Hooper revisits the founding question NSF posed in 2001 — how a major infrastructure investment could transform hydrologic science — and argues it's an opportune time to revisit that question given the uncertainty currently surrounding NSF funding.

[Read Rick Hooper's full reflection on cuahsi.org →](https://www.cuahsi.org/community/news/cuahsis-25th-anniversary-reflections-a-challenge-for-todays-hydrologic-scientists-by-rick-hooper)

Additional reflections from community members will continue through the remainder of the year.

### HydroLearn Workshop and Hackathon recap

This summer, educators, researchers, and hydrology professionals from across the US and Canada came together for a HydroLearn–CIROH Workshop and Hackathon. The event combined three days of in-person collaboration in Salt Lake City during the CIROH Developers Conference (May 27–29) with an intensive eight-day virtual hackathon, producing **11 new HydroLearn modules** showcasing research tools developed by the CIROH community.

**22 fellows** — research scientists, postdoctoral scholars, and graduate students — were paired and assigned to module topics aligned with their expertise, guided by education and hydrology experts to transform research into peer-reviewed learning modules now freely available through HydroLearn.

### UCOWR Annual Conference

Kimberlee Wong (Environmental Data Science Fellow) and Jordan Read (CEO) attended the Universities Council on Water Resources Annual Conference, June 7–10 in San Antonio — CUAHSI's second year participating. Activities included an exhibit booth, a talk from Jordan Read on CUAHSI, and a workshop on HydroShare and reproducibility.
