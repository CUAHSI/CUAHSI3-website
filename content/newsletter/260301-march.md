## Announcements

### Letter from the Chief Executive Officer

![Jordan Read, CEO of CUAHSI](/newsletter-images/2026-march/ceo-jordan-read.jpg)

**Jordan Read (he/him)**, CEO, CUAHSI


Twenty-five years ago this June, a group of scientists who had spent nearly two decades fighting for hydrology's place in the national research agenda had something big to show for it: a signed incorporation document for “CUAHSI” and 33 member universities ready to build something together. They couldn't have known exactly what this non-profit would become, but they knew the field needed it.

2026 is our 25th anniversary year, and we're going to mark it in ways that are worth paying attention to. Over the coming months we'll be releasing new work that tells the story of what this community has built: in numbers, in images, and in words. And we’ll make the case for what a water science community with 25 years of shared infrastructure behind it is now positioned to do. Some of what's coming will be familiar and I think some of it will surprise you.

This isn't a "look how far we've come" message. Instead, we’re showing that the infrastructure, the training programs, the data standards, and the building of community all set us up for what’s next. The water challenges ahead are larger than anything we've faced. Meeting them means navigating what AI is changing about environmental science, building a water science that speaks directly to human decisions and a human-altered planet, and cultivating the scientists prepared to lead in that era. This requires exactly the kind of foundation CUAHSI has spent 25 years building. We think the community is ready to break through, and we intend to help make that happen.

More details will follow throughout the year. For now, stay tuned. There's a lot worth watching in 2026.

Warm regards,
Jordan

### Apply to be an instructor for the 2026 Fall semester of the CUAHSI Virtual University!

![CUAHSI Virtual University: now accepting module proposals from instructors, submit by March 16, 2026](/newsletter-images/2026-march/cvu-instructor-call.jpg)

**Faculty**: apply [here](https://cuahsi.jotform.com/260414260402947) by March 16, 2026 to be an instructor for the CVU!

The CUAHSI Virtual University (CVU) is an educational program designed to expand access to specialized training in hydrology and water science. The program consists of self-contained, 4-week modules taught by faculty at participating institutions. Students at participating institutions can enroll in up to three modules and receive credit through their home institution. Faculty who teach modules serve as the instructor-of-record for students from their own institutions, enabling all students at their institution an opportunity to enroll and access all of the modules available through the CVU.

The CVU is designed to:​

- expand instructional impact by increasing reach,
- expose students to a subdiscipline or specialized training that is not otherwise available,
- broaden the academic network of participants, and
- enhance the depth and breadth of graduate course offerings of participating institutions.

Faculty participation includes:

- teaching a one-credit self contained course module over 4 weeks,
- serving as the instructor-of-record for students at their institution, and
- earning teaching credit through your home institution. ​

More details can be found on our [website](https://www.cuahsi.org/virtual-university).

If you have any questions or would like to request an extension for applying, please contact Julia Masterman, [jmasterman@cuahsi.org](mailto:jmasterman@cuahsi.org).

### Apply Now: WaterSoftHack 2026 Cyberinfrastructure and Machine Learning Training to Advance Water Science Research

July 20-31, 2026 (Virtual)

![WaterSoftHack logo](/newsletter-images/2026-march/watersofthack-logo.png)

WaterSoftHack is a collaborative effort led by Dr. Vidya Samadi, Clemson University, with the University of Iowa, Tulane University and CUAHSI. The initiative aims to cultivate the workforce that creates, utilizes, and supports advanced cyberinfrastructure (CI) workflows and tools to enable and potentially transform fundamental water science education and research. This is a multi-year NSF-funded project, dedicated to advancing water science research and education through the synergistic power of CI and machine learning training.

This year will focus on cloud and edge computing, equipping the next generation of water science and engineering researchers with cutting-edge technology and CI training. Participants will gain hands-on experience running machine learning models in cloud and edge computing environments, and learn how to design scalable workflows, deploy models efficiently, and leverage modern computational resources for water research applications.

Graduate and undergraduate students, postdocs, engineers, professionals, and early-career faculty are encouraged to apply. See the Voices from the Community section below to read reflections from a few of the 2025 Fellows.

[Apply now through March 31, 2026](https://watersofthack.github.io)

## Community Engagement

### CUAHSI Visits Hamilton College to Share Water Data Tools and Workflows

CUAHSI's Research Analyst, Abner Bogan, recently visited Hamilton College, a Primarily Undergraduate University (PUI) located in Clinton, NY at the invitation of faculty member Dr. Heather Kropp, to share data services and education resources at CUAHSI. During the visit, Abner led an interactive session demonstrating how to find data in HydroShare and analyze it using cloud computing tools for Dr. Kropp’s Environmental Data Science course. Students explored a water data workflow using USGS APIs that enabled them to enter their own location and find relevant local water data, which will support their final projects where they’ll need to find and use real environmental datasets. The workflow is documented in [this](https://hydroshare.org/resource/5249e9b2471941f1b12dad34c9ddf6fb/) HydroShare resource. Abner also presented an open science seminar for the broader Environmental Studies seminar, which included students and faculty with both science and humanities backgrounds. The visit highlighted how CUAHSI’s data tools can be integrated into undergraduate classrooms and introduced a new group of students to the data and resources available through CUAHSI.

Interested in bringing CUAHSI tools or training into your classroom or campus community? As part of our ongoing efforts to engage institutions across the water science community, we welcome opportunities to connect with faculty and students through virtual or in-person seminar presentations, workshops, or interactive demonstrations. Reach out to [connect@cuahsi.org](mailto:connect@cuahsi.org?subject=&body=) to learn more.
