## Letter from the Chief Executive Officer

*Jordan Read (he/him), CEO, CUAHSI*

Twenty-five years ago this June, a group of scientists who had spent nearly two decades fighting for hydrology's place in the national research agenda had something big to show for it: a signed incorporation document for "CUAHSI" and 33 member universities ready to build something together. They couldn't have known exactly what this non-profit would become, but they knew the field needed it.

2026 is our 25th anniversary year, and we're going to mark it in ways that are worth paying attention to. Over the coming months we'll be releasing new work that tells the story of what this community has built: in numbers, in images, and in words. And we'll make the case for what a water science community with 25 years of shared infrastructure behind it is now positioned to do.

This isn't a "look how far we've come" message. Instead, we're showing that the infrastructure, the training programs, the data standards, and the building of community all set us up for what's next. The water challenges ahead are larger than anything we've faced. Meeting them means navigating what AI is changing about environmental science, building a water science that speaks directly to human decisions and a human-altered planet, and cultivating the scientists prepared to lead in that era. This requires exactly the kind of foundation CUAHSI has spent 25 years building. We think the community is ready to break through, and we intend to help make that happen.

More details will follow throughout the year. For now, stay tuned. There's a lot worth watching in 2026.

### Apply to be an instructor for the 2026 Fall CUAHSI Virtual University

Faculty: apply by March 16, 2026 to be an instructor for the CVU. The CUAHSI Virtual University (CVU) is an educational program designed to expand access to specialized training in hydrology and water science, consisting of self-contained 4-week modules taught by faculty at participating institutions. Students at participating institutions can enroll in up to three modules and receive credit through their home institution.

## News from the board

Members of CUAHSI's Board of Directors are shaping the future of hydrologic science. This month, we spotlight **Tao Wen**, a newly elected board member whose research bridges hydrology, geochemistry, and data science to uncover how water and solutes move through Earth systems.

Tao Wen is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences at Syracuse University. He has been a key contributor to community data platforms like the Shale Network and GRID-DB, which make large-scale environmental datasets openly accessible to researchers across disciplines. As a new member of the CUAHSI Board of Directors, he brings both scientific expertise and a commitment to open, collaborative infrastructure for hydrologic science.

## Voices from the community: WaterSoftHack fellows share their experience

Clemson University, in partnership with the University of Iowa, Tulane University, and CUAHSI, is continuing to grow WaterSoftHack as the program enters its third year. CUAHSI connected with a few of last year's fellows to hear about their experiences:

- **Moses Kiwanuka**, PhD Candidate, Earth Systems Science, Florida International University
- **Aashish Gautam**, Graduate Research Assistant, Jackson State University
- **Hassan Saleh**, PhD Candidate, Geosciences, Western Michigan University

Fellows shared what drew them to the workshop, the skills and training they gained, and how they plan to apply these methods in their research. They also reflected on the collaborative opportunities the workshop provided and how it has helped expand their networks within the water science community.

## Community engagement

### CUAHSI visits Hamilton College

CUAHSI's Research Analyst, Abner Bogan, recently visited Hamilton College, a Primarily Undergraduate University in Clinton, NY, at the invitation of faculty member Dr. Heather Kropp. Abner led an interactive session demonstrating how to find data in HydroShare and analyze it using cloud computing tools for Dr. Kropp's Environmental Data Science course. Students explored a water data workflow using USGS APIs that enabled them to enter their own location and find relevant local water data. Abner also presented an open science seminar for the broader Environmental Studies seminar.

The visit highlighted how CUAHSI's data tools can be integrated into undergraduate classrooms and introduced a new group of students to the resources available through CUAHSI.

*Interested in bringing CUAHSI tools or training into your classroom or campus community? Reach out to connect@cuahsi.org.*
