## Letter from the CEO

![Jordan Read, CEO of CUAHSI](/newsletter-images/2026-november/ceo-jordan-read.jpg)

**Jordan Read**, CEO, CUAHSI

This is a sneaky-busy time of year. Today we kick off our first-ever Water Science Exchange with partners at the University of Missouri, and staff are preparing for a large presence at the AGU Fall Meeting (see below for more details). I know you are busy too, balancing growing demands on your time and looking toward a restful stretch that always seems just around the corner but routinely gives way to surprise priorities.

CUAHSI builds community capacity for water science, which is paradoxically becoming easier and harder in this increasingly digital and rapidly changing science environment. It is easier to reach people but harder to build and keep authentic connections. Those connections are what matter most for moving the field forward. CUAHSI is 25 this year, and its most valuable work has come from people in this community connecting with each other. Make time for that this season, and not just with CUAHSI staff. Catch up with a colleague you haven't talked to in a while. At AGU, introduce yourself to someone outside your field. Please do the same where you live.

### CUAHSI Water Learning Commons

![Water Learning Commons](/newsletter-images/2026-november/water-learning-commons-logo.png)

The [CUAHSI Water Learning Commons](https://water-learning-commons.cuahsi.org/) is a free, open collection of water science teaching materials made by and for the community: full courses, code notebooks, and workshop activities you can use or adapt. It is built so instructors can reuse each other's materials and authors get credit and can see how their work is used.

The current collection of materials includes **JP Gannon's** Hydroinformatics course, **Mark Bakker** and **Vincent Post's** Analytical Groundwater Modeling course, **Keira Johnson's** introductory hydrology workshop using an interactive app called HydroViz, and several CUAHSI-curated notebooks containing reusable workflows with open water data.

Everything is openly licensed, and resources hosted directly on the platform are archived and citable via HydroShare. In the coming months we'll add field protocols, reproducible workflow examples, and real-world impact stories.

[Browse the Water Learning Commons](https://water-learning-commons.cuahsi.org/)

**Share your teaching materials.** If you've built courseware, field protocols, notebooks, or other teaching materials others could use, we'd like to host them and help you reach more people. You keep ownership and can keep working where you already do. Authors also receive an Authorship Impact Report showing how the community uses their materials, useful for planning revisions and for CVs or annual reviews.

To add your materials or learn more, email help@cuahsi.org with a link and a short description.

### CUAHSI Joins New Partnership to Expand Near-Surface Geophysics Support

![A full room listens to a plenary talk; the screen shows the logos of the partner organizations, including NGF, CUAHSI, NHERI and OpenTopography](/newsletter-images/2026-november/near-surface-geophysics-workshop-plenary.jpg) ![Attendees talk around a table covered with paper and sticky notes during a breakout session](/newsletter-images/2026-november/near-surface-geophysics-workshop-breakout.jpg)

*Photos above: August Near-surface Geophysics Community Input Workshop, Beth Pratt-Sitaula*

[Near-surface geophysics](https://www.earthscope.org/what-is/near-surface-geophysics/) methods have broad applicability in a variety of geoscience and environmental disciplines such as hydrology, critical zone science, and engineering geology. Until now, though, there has not been a unified center to support research and education in near-surface geophysics. Five NSF-funded Earth science organizations involved in aspects of near-surface geophysics have entered a new partnership to centralize support; these organizations are the [Center for Transformative Environmental Monitoring Programs](https://ctemps.org/) (CTEMPs), [Natural Hazards Engineering Research Infrastructure](https://www.designsafe-ci.org/about/) (NHERI), the Consortium of Universities for the Advancement of Hydrologic Science, Inc. (CUAHSI), [OpenTopography](https://opentopography.org/), and the [NSF National Geophysical Facility](https://ngf.earthscope.org/) (NSF NGF operated by EarthScope).

The first activity taken together was the [Near-surface Geophysics Community Input Workshop](https://ngf.earthscope.org/events/2026-near-surface-geophysics-community-input-workshop), bringing together a broad cross-section of the geoscience and near-surface geophysics community. Developed with input from the AGU Near-surface Geophysics Section and held in late August 2026 in Albuquerque, New Mexico, the workshop, attended by CUAHSI staff **Jordan Read**, CEO, and **Julia Masterman**, Community Relations and Training Coordinator, aimed to fortify relationships, surface new collaborations, and build on prior community input.

Ninety-four attendees, ranging from academic faculty, researchers, and late-stage graduate students to industry professionals and federal employees, were tasked with assessing gaps, identifying complementary efforts, and considering how to coordinate opportunities among the partner organizations and the broader research and education communities. Across a day and a half of plenary talks and interactive breakout sessions, attendees identified high priority actions to increase collective impact.

A synthesis of workshop outcomes is now available, which drew from the community input to create a set of prioritized capabilities for partner organizations to follow. In the near term, the organizations will implement the partnership and virtual center governance structure and create an online landing page for sharing resources. Thereafter, the partners plan to work together to sponsor more community events, acquire additional instrumentation through current funds and new proposals, develop data tools and best practices, build a portfolio of multi-modal training programs, and create mechanisms for interdisciplinary networking and collaboration.

[View the report here.](/documents/news/2026-near-surface-geophysics-workshop-report.pdf)

### 2026 Kenneth J. Lanfear Award for Outstanding Technology Paper

Congratulations to the authors of "[Machine Learning for a Heterogeneous Water Modeling Framework](https://doi.org/10.1111/1752-1688.70000)," recipient of the 2026 [Kenneth J. Lanfear Award for Outstanding Technology Paper](https://www.awra.org/AWRA/Members/Scholarship_and_Awards/Award_Descriptions/Kenneth_Lanfear_Award.aspx) in the *Journal of the American Water Resources Association*. The award is given annually by the American Water Resources Association. The paper grew out of research spanning three Water Prediction Innovators Summer Institutes (2021-2023). Several of the authors are Summer Institute alumni, who have since completed their degrees and now work as assistant professors, at NASA and NOAA, as postdocs, and at private engineering firms.

Authors: Jonathan M. Frame, Ryoko Araki, Soelem Aafnan Bhuiyan, Tadd Bindas, Jeremy Rapp, Lauren Bolotin, Emily Deardorff, Qiyue Liu, Francisco Haces-Garcia, Mochi Liao, Nels Frazier, and Fred L. Ogden.

### CyberWater2 Training: Advancing Hydrologic Modeling Through Scientific Workflows

![Portraits of the CyberWater2 project team and instructors, with the logos of the University of Pittsburgh, Indiana University Indianapolis, CyberWater and CUAHSI](/newsletter-images/2026-november/cyberwater2-training-team.jpg)

In September and October 2026, CUAHSI and the CyberWater2 project team at the University of Pittsburgh and Indiana University Indianapolis ran a virtual training on [CyberWater](https://cyber-water.luddy.indianapolis.iu.edu/), a framework for linking models, data, and computing tools into reproducible hydrologic modeling workflows. Forty-seven participants from at least 24 institutions in five countries participated.

Through live demonstrations, discussions, and hands-on exercises, participants built and ran data-intensive modeling workflows. They could also draft and share a short proposal for using CyberWater in their own research or extending it with other models and tools.

Thank you to the CyberWater2 project team, instructors, and participants. Want to learn more about CyberWater? Lecture recordings are available free on the CyberWater page of the [CUAHSI website](https://www.cuahsi.org/cyberwater) and the CyberWater playlist on [CUAHSI's YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLCBF49uU4qHM) channel.
