## Announcements

![AGU25, New Orleans, LA, 15–19 December 2025](/newsletter-images/2026-january/agu25-banner.png)

CUAHSI participated in the American Geophysical Union (AGU) Annual Meeting, held December 15–19 in New Orleans, Louisiana. Throughout the week, CUAHSI staff connected with members of the water science community, gathering valuable feedback and discussing ways to better support research, education, and professional development.

A highlight of the week was CUAHSI’s community gathering on the evening of December 17 at Ernst Café, which brought **164 attendees** together for appetizers, games, and informal networking. Later that evening, **29 attendees** joined the Water Prediction Innovators Summer Institute Community Gathering to reconnect with alumni, learn more about the program, and engage with others working in water prediction and related fields.

**Nine CUAHSI staff** attended the meeting, contributing to [**18 presentations**](https://www.cuahsi.org/events/2025-american-geophysical-union-annual-meeting) through panels, posters, and talks. The CUAHSI booth in the exhibit hall served as a central hub where attendees could connect with staff to learn more about CUAHSI’s programs and services. Staff led live demonstrations of HydroShare, the CUAHSI booth was featured as a stop on an exhibit hall tour where a small group stopped by to learn more about data management, and visitors were invited to mark their institutional facilities with a pin on a world map, which quickly filled with locations from around the world.

We greatly enjoyed seeing so many of you at AGU, and we look forward to continued opportunities to connect in 2026.

### HydroLearn-CIROH May/June 2026 Workshop and Hackathon

![HydroLearn logo](/newsletter-images/2026-january/hydrolearn-logo.png)

![CIROH (Cooperative Institute for Research to Operations in Hydrology) logo](/newsletter-images/2026-january/ciroh-logo.png)

The HydroLearn team is pleased to invite faculty, postdoctoral scholars, and graduate students with expertise in hydrology to participate in the 2026 HydroLearn-CIROH Workshop & Hackathon, to be held in May/June 2026.

**The deadline to apply is January 31, 2026**

**Event Dates:** In-person workshop May 27 - 29 and virtual hackathon June 2 - 5 and June 8 - 11, 2026

Selected participants will receive travel support to attend the CIROH Developers Conference in Salt Lake City, UT (May 27 - 29, 2026) to collaborate with researchers and practitioners advancing river forecasting models and complete the in-person portion of our training. Participants will then work in pairs to create a high-quality learning module through our guided 8-day virtual hackathon (June 2 - 5 and June 8 - 11, 2026) with hydrology and education experts. Upon completion and peer review, modules will be hosted on the HydroLearn platform.

### About HydroLearn

Originally supported by the National Science Foundation in 2008, [HydroLearn](https://hydrolearn.org/) is a platform and pedagogical model that integrates best practices in course design into workshops and hackathons to train hydrology educators and produce high-quality open online resources. The platform already contains over 60 peer-reviewed modules co-created by past HydroLearn fellows on a wide range of topics.

Visit the Hackathon [website](https://www.cuahsi.org/workshops/hydrolearn-ciroh-may-june-2026-workshop-and-hackathon) for information on eligibility, participant support, module topics, and more.

[LEARN MORE & APPLY HERE](https://www.cuahsi.org/workshops/hydrolearn-ciroh-may-june-2026-workshop-and-hackathon)

## Voices From the Community

### Masoumeh Hashemi

![Masoumeh Hashemi](/newsletter-images/2026-january/masoumeh-hashemi.jpg)

Graduate Research Assistant

Plants, Soils & Climate Department

S.J. & Jessie E. Quinney College of Agriculture & Natural Resources

Utah State University

This month’s Community Guest Spotlight features **Masoumeh Hashemi**, a Graduate Research Assistant in the Plants, Soils & Climate Department at Utah State University whose work addresses real-world water challenges. Her research focuses on improving water measurement in irrigation canals and ditches using low-cost, camera-based approaches powered by deep learning and image processing. By advancing more accessible, real-time monitoring methods, Masoumeh’s work supports better decision-making for farmers, canal companies, and water managers in water-scarce regions. [**Read the full article here**](https://www.cuahsi.org/community/news/january-e-newsletter-community-guest-spotlight-with-masoumeh-hashemi) to learn more about Masoumeh’s work and journey in water science.

## HydroShare

**HydroShare Data Spotlight:** Each month we highlight a published HydroShare resource that ranks within the top 10 most visited published resources over the past 30 days.

This month's featured resource contains datasets, code, and modeling results supporting the paper an [*Analysis of Fixture Efficiency and Behavioral Factors of Indoor Residential Water Use of Single-Family Households*](https://www.hydroshare.org/resource/3dce7ad4d8314436b1c22d66a5979590/)*.* The resource enables researchers to explore factors differentiating high and low water-using households, the relative importance of fixture efficiency versus behavior, and the effects of household size and weather variations on residential water consumption patterns across more than 33,000 households throughout the US.
