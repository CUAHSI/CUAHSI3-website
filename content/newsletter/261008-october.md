## CUAHSI celebrates 25 years

As part of CUAHSI's 25th anniversary, we're featuring a series of reflections from some of the people who have helped shape CUAHSI's story over the years. Our next invited contributor is JP Gannon, CUAHSI Board of Directors, Chair.

**JP Gannon**, Associate Professor, Virginia Tech

CUAHSI has been a part of my academic journey since I was a PhD student. My first interaction with CUAHSI was probably attending a mixer at AGU around 2011. Throughout my career my interactions with CUAHSI have evolved. It went from a way to find other water scientists, to a funding source, to a place to preserve data (HydroShare!), to a way to expand my work on hydrology education, and now as an organization I'm happy to serve in any way I can. As chair of the Board of Directors, I have an even fuller appreciation for CUAHSI and the hard work of its employees, CEO, and Board, and I'm hopeful that it continues long into the future so others can continue to benefit.

I learned through serving on the board that the existence of CUAHSI is not a given. A LOT of community support and hard work keeps CUAHSI running. So, if I can use my spot in the newsletter to advocate: even though CUAHSI is crushing it right now, we can't lose sight of the need for support. When decisions are being made by funders, we want it to be obvious that CUAHSI is critical. There are many ways you can support CUAHSI! Join or renew your institution's membership, forward the newsletter or announcements to colleagues, send an email to new students or faculty introducing them to CUAHSI, nominate yourself for the Board or an advisory committee, or a colleague for an award, participate in one of CUAHSI's programs, or host some data on HydroShare!

Cheers to CUAHSI's 25th anniversary. I look forward to showing my support well after my time on the board comes to a close!

[Read the full reflection on our website.](https://www.cuahsi.org/community/news/cuahsis-25th-anniversary-reflections-with-jp-gannon)

### CUAHSI Skills Certificate Pilot

**Reproducible Workflows with Open Water Data: NASA, NOAA, USGS River Data Access**

**Date:** November 3 to December 4, 2026 | **Location:** Virtual and live sessions | **Registration deadline:** October 23, 2026

Water science researchers rely on datasets from federal agencies, but few have been trained in how to access this data using recommended and sustainable patterns. This Python-based course builds data literacy skills for programmatically accessing datasets from NASA, NOAA, and USGS, and understanding the strengths of each in water research applications. This pilot course has been developed with the recommendations of the data providers themselves. This pilot training course will cover the following data sets:

- NASA [Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT)](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/swot/)
- NOAA [National Water Model (NWM)](https://water.noaa.gov/about/nwm)
- USGS [Water Data for the Nation (WDFN)](https://waterdata.usgs.gov)

This course is designed for graduate students, postdocs, early-career researchers, and professionals who use federal water data in their research and have basic experience using Python.

The course will consist of live virtual training sessions coupled with asynchronous assignments, totaling 20 hours of participation. Participants who complete the course and successfully demonstrate their understanding will receive a certificate of completion.

[Learn more and register for the pilot training course](https://www.cuahsi.org/workshops/cuahsi-skills-certificate-pilot-reproducible-workflows-with-open-water-data-nasa-noaa-usgs-river-data-access)

### Water Data Forum: Durable Skills for the Water Workforce in the Age of AI

**November 4, 2026 | 12:00 pm to 1:00 pm ET**

In this free webinar, researchers, managers, and workforce development experts will examine which analytical and modeling skills remain durable, and why demand is growing for people who can check what AI produces, direct the work of AI alongside the work of people, and explain results to those who act on them. Panelists will also discuss what this means for hiring, for people already in water jobs, and for the pipeline of water sector talent. Jordan Read, Chief Executive Officer at CUAHSI, will moderate the panel.

Speakers include:

- Dr. Alison Appling, Ecologist at U.S. Geological Survey
- John Ikeda, Chief Mission Officer at Water Environment Federation

The Water Data Forum is a free, interactive web series convened by Cleveland Water Alliance, the Water Environment Federation, and CUAHSI to demystify topics of water data for a broad professional audience. Topics range from specific technologies to broader application spaces, exploring the full breadth of the world of water data.

## Announcements

Punwath Prum, a Graduate Student Researcher at the University of Pittsburgh, has launched the SWOT River Discharge Explorer, a web application for exploring, visualizing, and downloading river discharge time series for North American rivers. Built on satellite-derived data from the Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) mission, the Hydrocron service from NASA's Physical Oceanography Distributed Active Archive Center (PO.DAAC), and the SWOT River Database (SWORD) river network, the tool supports researchers and water resource professionals in identifying hydrologic trends and understanding how rivers respond to environmental change. [Learn more about the SWOT River Discharge Explorer.](https://punwath.github.io/swot-river-discharge-explorer/)

### Member spotlight

CUAHSI member [American Institute of Hydrology (AIH)](https://www.linkedin.com/company/aihydrology/) is offering the AIH Fundamental Exam to qualified students and early-career professionals with no upfront exam or application fees from September to December 2026. It's an opportunity to demonstrate your knowledge, begin building your professional credentials, and take an important step toward becoming an AIH Hydrologist-in-Training (HIT). If you pass the Part I (Fundamentals) exam and choose to accept your AIH HIT certification, the exam fee will be discounted 50%. If you do not pass, you pay no exam fee. [Learn more about the exam on the AIH website.](https://aihydrology.org/youre-ready-take-the-exam/)
