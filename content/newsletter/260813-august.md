## Letter from the Chief Executive Officer

![Jordan Read, CEO of CUAHSI](/newsletter-images/2026-august/ceo-jordan-read.jpg)

**Jordan Read (he/him)**, CEO, CUAHSI

[Rick's reflection last month](https://www.cuahsi.org/community/news/cuahsi-e-newsletter-july-2026) asked what hydrologic science needs in order to advance, and his question was timely. The answer in 2001 came from a community that had to organize itself to respond, and four ideas emerged. One of the ideas evolved into the water data services CUAHSI runs today, and the other three did not survive.

The surviving idea solved an immediate problem that was projected to compound. Researchers needed somewhere to put data and a common way to describe it in order to unlock opportunities for more impactful science. It was known at the time that the scale of water data, the demands on computing, and the need to work across disciplinary boundaries would all grow. Some of that early work led to standards this community developed that now sit underneath NOAA's water forecasting and USGS's monitoring locations. That is a deeper societal impact than anyone promised in 2001.

I think addressing this question now is harder for us, partly because it is less clear who we are answering to. Federal priorities are shifting quickly and funding is shrinking. AI is changing what a research community needs from shared infrastructure, and is eroding some of the ways researchers find each other and work together. A lot of what CUAHSI built over the last 25 years will matter more in this environment. Some of it will matter less. I see our challenge as bringing this water community together and helping it align on work that matters, at a time when the field is dispersing and institutional bonds are thinning.

Over the remainder of the year we will be creating more places for this conversation to evolve, and we'd love to hear from you.

Regards,
Jordan

## CUAHSI celebrates 25 years

### Convening the Community in the New Era of Water Science

![Hoori Ajami](/newsletter-images/2026-august/hoori-ajami.jpg)

As part of CUAHSI's 25th anniversary, we're featuring a series of reflections from some of the people who have helped shape CUAHSI's story over the years. Our next invited contributor is **Hoori Ajami**, CUAHSI Board of Directors, Chair-Elect 2027.

I first heard about CUAHSI as a graduate student at the University of Arizona, during my first AGU Fall meeting in 2007. My co-mentor, the late Professor Thomas Meixner, always reminded us that Tuesday evening was reserved for the Hydrology Section and CUAHSI reception, and he strongly encouraged us to attend. I remember going to that first reception and feeling inspired hearing about the latest developments and opportunities within the hydrologic science community, and what opportunities CUAHSI offered. AGU meetings are huge, so having a more focused networking event, one that put real emphasis on graduate student engagement, meant a great deal to me. This event quickly became a tradition, and I made sure to attend every year after that.

I became formally involved with CUAHSI in 2021, as a faculty member at the University of California, Riverside, when I joined CUAHSI's Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) committee, which was new at the time. As part of the Committee's initiatives, I worked with colleagues across the country to revisit the criteria for some of CUAHSI's student grants with the intention of increasing engagement with underrepresented groups in the water science community.

That work led to an invitation to join the Board of Directors, and I became a Board member in January 2023. I eagerly accepted this opportunity to serve our community as I deeply believe in CUAHSI's mission. As a faculty member, I had already benefited from CUAHSI's webinar series, long-form lectures on the latest research in our field, recorded and made freely accessible to the community. Furthermore, during the challenges of the pandemic, the teaching materials generously shared by members of our community via CUAHSI platforms became an invaluable resource for navigating the sudden shift to online instruction. My first Board task was contributing to CUAHSI's strategic plan, a two-day exercise dedicated to thinking deeply about the future of water science and how CUAHSI could help shape it. Since then, I've served as a Board of Directors liaison for the Education and Outreach Committee, worked on revising CUAHSI's governance and bylaws, and served on the Executive Committee. This journey has shown me how expansive CUAHSI's impact is beyond education. Especially as funding environments shift, our Board spends real time thinking about how to keep organizations like CUAHSI sustainable, and how to bring more of the community into that conversation.

Please continue reading [Hoori Ajami's article here](https://www.cuahsi.org/community/news/cuahsis-25th-anniversary-reflections-convening-the-community-in-the-new-era-of-water-science-by-hoori-ajami) and keep an eye out for additional reflections through the remainder of the year.

### Applications Are Now Open for the CyberWater2 Workshop Event

[![CyberWater](/newsletter-images/2026-august/cyberwater2-logo.png)](https://cyber-water.luddy.indianapolis.iu.edu/)

[Apply here by August 28th.](https://cuahsi.jotform.com/262093687207867)

The [CyberWater](https://cyber-water.luddy.indianapolis.iu.edu/)2 workshop is a hands-on model training program led by the University of Pittsburgh, Indiana University Indianapolis, and CUAHSI. Participants will learn how to build, run, and adapt water science workflows in CyberWater through guided demonstrations and practical activities.

The trainings will be held the week of September 21-25, 2026. Participants will attend live virtual sessions on Monday (9/21) and Wednesday (9/23), complete self-paced training exercises throughout the week, and reconvene for a final check-in on Friday (9/25).

There are two training tracks to choose from:

1. Training + Certificate/Stipend: attend all training events and work to complete extra, asynchronous hands-on training exercises to be eligible to receive a $200 participant stipend.
2. Training Only: attend the training events only.

The workshop is designed to help participants become productive CyberWater users by emphasizing reproducible workflows, model integration, and scientific problem solving. The specific learning objectives for participants in this workshop are the following:

- Learn how to navigate CyberWater interface and build workflows
- Learn how to run a model simulation in CyberWater
- Learn the components of the generic model agent toolkit and model integration
- Learn how to use CyberWater to answer new scientific questions

Applications will close on Aug 28, 2026. Visit the [website](https://www.cuahsi.org/events/cyberwater2-training) for more details and reach out to [Danielle Tijerina-Kreuzer](mailto:dtijerina@cuahsi.org) with questions.

### New Publication from the CZNet Synthesis Working Group

![CZNet: Critical Zone Collaborative Network](/newsletter-images/2026-august/cznet-logo.png)

A new paper was published in Earth's Future, ["Dominant Controls on Preferential Flow and Their Implications for Future Soil Water Fluxes,"](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2026EF008296) by lead author Bonan Li, Associate Professor, Northeast Normal University. The study uses a deep learning model trained on data from 33 U.S. sites to predict when and how quickly preferential flow occurs, finding that precipitation characteristics and soil clay content are key drivers, with climate projections suggesting this flow will speed up in the future. This research was supported through a synthesis project funded by the CZNet Hub, housed at CUAHSI, which convenes teams of scientists at the John Wesley Powell Center for Analysis and Synthesis to address Critical Zone science questions that cross disciplinary and site boundaries.

### Water Prediction Innovators Summer Institute Capstone Event

The [Water Prediction Innovators Summer Institute](https://www.cuahsi.org/summer-institute) (WPI SI) Capstone took place July 22, 2026 at The University of Alabama in Tuscaloosa. A collaboration between CUAHSI, The University of Alabama, Alabama Water Institute, and NOAA's Office of Water Prediction, the WPI SI is a seven-week hands-on program that trains graduate students in tools and methods related to water prediction. Now in its 11th year, the WPI SI draws 100+ applicants annually, with fellows and alumni representing 100+ universities.

The 2026 Water Prediction Innovators Summer Institute took place June 8 - July 22, 2026. 24 graduate student fellows from 21 unique U.S. institutions formed research teams under the guidance of seven Theme Leaders, spanning topics from precipitation forecasting to flood risk communication. Research outcomes are summarized in the [2026 WPI SI final report](https://www.hydroshare.org/resource/7b2b22d88a914bb79b3f48e5ea3ecaaf/).

CEO Jordan Read and Community Relations and Training Coordinator Julia Masterman attended the Capstone event where fellows presented their research to an audience that included representatives from the NOAA Office of Water Prediction, CIROH, and The University of Alabama, as well as the fellows' advisors and peers.

Congratulations to the 2026 WPI SI student fellows, Theme Leaders, Course Coordinators, and all project team members and mentors on another successful program!
