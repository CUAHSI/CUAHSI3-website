## Announcements

### CUAHSI's 25th anniversary celebration

Last month, we shared that 2026 marks CUAHSI's 25th anniversary. A lot has happened since CUAHSI was formed in 2001. CUAHSI has grown into a central resource for the water science community, expanding access to data, training, and research infrastructure. HydroShare has become a widely used platform for managing, sharing, and publishing water data, with more than 25,000 registered users across water, climate, and geohazards research. Our Cyberseminar program, launched in 2003 and still running today, has made expert science topics freely accessible, with hundreds of presentations now archived on our YouTube channel.

Through the rest of the year, we'll be sharing stories, milestones, and reflections that highlight what investments in CUAHSI have made possible. **We can't tell the full story without hearing from you** — the people who use our programs and services.

So, we're asking to hear from you: what's a CUAHSI moment that stands out?

- Was there a conversation at a CUAHSI meeting that opened a door or sparked a collaboration you didn't expect?
- Has HydroShare helped you publish or manage data in a way that made a difference for your research?
- Did you learn something at a workshop or training that has had a lasting impact on your research or work?

Send your reflections, memories, or photos to commgr@cuahsi.org.

## Community engagement

### University of Florida Water Institute's Spring Student Showcase

CUAHSI Environmental Data Science Fellow Kimmy Wong attended the UF Water Institute's Spring Student Showcase in Gainesville, FL, March 25–26. This year's skill-building workshop featured a two-hour session on water data services. Kimmy led a session introducing attendees to HydroShare and associated tools for data archiving and discovery. The workshop drew an enthusiastic response from UF's graduate student community.

### Science Talk '26

CUAHSI Community Outreach Specialist Lisa Mucciacito attended the Association of Science Communicators Science Talk '26 conference in Portland, OR, March 26–27 and presented a poster titled "From Data to Connection: CUAHSI's Member Engagement Reports as a Model for Science Communication." Key themes throughout the conference included the evolving role of AI in science communication, the importance of human oversight in maintaining content accuracy and credibility, and strategies for building public trust in science.

### National Model Evaluation Hackathon — March 30–April 1, 2026 | Princeton, NJ

CUAHSI research team members Danielle Tijerina-Kreuzer, Irene Garousi-Nejad, and Tony Castronova, in collaboration with researchers at Princeton University and University of Arizona, co-organized a hydrologic model evaluation Hackathon. The event brought together hydrologic modelers and researchers to co-design an open evaluation framework.

Participants used Jupyter Notebook workflows leveraging the HydroData data catalog API to evaluate model outputs (e.g., ParFlow-CONUS and National Water Model) against observational datasets (e.g., USGS streamflow, snow data). A key outcome was the successful use of the framework to evaluate streamflow from a participant's VIC model, as well as initial integrations of the SUMMA and iSnowbal models to evaluate snow outputs.

The Hackathon reinforced strong interest in a community-driven, interoperable evaluation framework that can support diverse models, datasets, and scientific questions.

## HydroShare

### HydroShare Discover page updates

HydroShare launched an updated Discover page to make it easier to find data and other resources. The new interface includes improved search across metadata (title, abstract, authors, keywords), autocomplete suggestions, and quick previews of resources directly in the results table.

### HydroShare resource spotlight

This month's spotlight is a collection containing the **National Ecological Observatory Network's (NEON) Hydrologic Data Products Site Level Resources**. This collection contains one resource for each NEON site, providing information on the hydrologic data products offered at that site across observational, instrumented, and remote sensing methods. Developed to support CUAHSI's Introduction to NEON for Hydrology cyberseminar series, this collection enables NEON's data to be discovered alongside other data in HydroShare.
