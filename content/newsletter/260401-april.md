## Announcements

### CUAHSI’s 25th Anniversary Celebration

![CUAHSI celebrating 25 years logo](/newsletter-images/2026-april/celebrating-25-years-logo.png)

Last month, we shared that 2026 marks CUAHSI's 25th anniversary!

A lot has happened since CUAHSI was formed in 2001. CUAHSI has grown into a central resource for the water science community, expanding access to data, training, and research infrastructure along the way. HydroShare has become a widely used platform for managing, sharing, and publishing water data, with more than 25,000 registered users across water, climate, and geohazards research. Through the years, CUAHSI’s meetings and events have brought people together and initiated collaborations, and our workshops have provided essential skill-building opportunities for researchers and students. Our Cyberseminar program, launched in 2003 and still running today, has made expert science topics freely accessible, with hundreds of presentations now archived on our [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@CUAHSI).

We mentioned that through the rest of the year, we'll be sharing stories, milestones, and reflections that highlight what investments in CUAHSI have made possible. We're working on some exciting plans, but the truth is, **we can’t tell the full story without hearing from you** - the people who use our programs and services and have helped shape our initiatives for the shared goal of advancing water science.

**So, we’re asking to hear from you:** what’s a CUAHSI moment that stands out?

- Was there a conversation at a CUAHSI meeting that opened a door or sparked a collaboration you didn't expect?

- Has HydroShare helped you publish or manage data in a way that made a difference for your research?

- Did you learn something at a workshop or training that has had a lasting impact on your research or work?

Send your reflections, memories, or photos to [commgr@cuahsi.org](mailto:commgr@cuahsi.org?subject=&body=) for a chance to be featured in our anniversary reflections throughout the year.

And stay tuned for details on opportunities to gather in person later in 2026.

### University of Florida Water Institute's Spring Student Showcase

CUAHSI Environmental Data Science Fellow Kimmy Wong attended the *University of Florida (UF) Water Institute's Spring Student Showcase* in Gainesville, FL, March 25-26. The semi-annual showcase featured student research presentations and faculty fellow award lectures. This year's skill-building workshop featured a two-hour session on water data services. Kimmy led a session which introduced attendees to HydroShare and associated tools for data archiving and discovery, with virtual support from Martin Seul.

The workshop drew an enthusiastic response from UF's graduate student community. Participants shared that even without data ready to upload, they felt confident knowing where to turn when the time came.

Interested in bringing CUAHSI tools or training into your classroom or campus community? As part of our ongoing efforts to engage institutions across the water science community, we welcome opportunities to connect with faculty and students through virtual or in-person seminar presentations, workshops, or interactive demonstrations. Reach out to [connect@cuahsi.org](mailto:connect@cuahsi.org?subject=&body=) to learn more.

### Science Talk ‘26

CUAHSI Community Outreach Specialist Lisa Mucciacito attended the *Association of Science Communicators Science Talk '26 conference* in Portland, OR, March 26–27 and presented a poster titled “From Data to Connection: CUAHSI's Member Engagement Reports as a Model for Science Communication.” The poster highlighted how CUAHSI’s annual engagement reports translate member activity data into accessible, visual summaries for affiliated institutions and enable members to see at a glance how people from their institutions interact with CUAHSI’s programs and services.

Key themes throughout the conference included the evolving role of AI in science communication, the importance of human oversight in maintaining content accuracy and credibility, and strategies for building public trust in science.

### National Model Evaluation Hackathon - March 30–April 1, 2026 | Princeton, NJ

Evaluating hydrologic models against observational data in open and reproducible ways is an ongoing challenge across hydrology, research, operations, and education. The NSF-funded Cyberinfrastructure for Sustainable Scientific Innovations (CSSI) Model Evaluation Framework project aims to address this by building a shared, open framework for model evaluation.

As part of this project, CUAHSI research team members Danielle Tijerina-Kreuzer, Irene Garousi-Nejad, and Tony Castronova, in collaboration with researchers at Princeton University and University of Arizona, co-organized a hydrologic model evaluation Hackathon. The event brought together hydrologic modelers and researchers to co-design the evaluation framework, inviting participants to work directly with early versions, share feedback, and play a central role in shaping its future direction. The Hackathon combined strategic discussions with interactive hackathon activities to introduce the framework, test its capabilities, and gather structured feedback from a diverse modeling community.

Participants used [Jupyter Notebook workflows](https://github.com/hydroframe/cssi_evaluation/blob/main/examples/nwm/nwm_swe_point_scale_evaluation.ipynb) leveraging the HydroData data catalog API to evaluate model outputs (e.g., ParFlow-CONUS and National Water Model) against observational datasets (e.g., USGS streamflow, snow data), and engaged in activities focused on metrics, data structures, and integrating additional models to the framework. A key outcome was the successful use of the current framework to evaluate streamflow from a participant’s VIC model, as well as initial integrations of the SUMMA and iSnowbal models to evaluate snow outputs. The Hackathon also generated community feedback on the model evaluation framework usability, standardization (data formats, units, metadata), and needed features such as spatial evaluation and improved documentation.

The Hackathon reinforced strong interest in a community-driven, interoperable evaluation framework that can support diverse models, datasets, and scientific questions.

### Additional Highlights

CUAHSI CEO Jordan Read traveled to Tuscaloosa, AL in early March to participate in the *Cooperative Institute for Research to Operations in Hydrology (CIROH) 5-Year Renewal Evaluation*, a formal review conducted by an external panel selected by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration. CUAHSI has been a partner in CIROH since its founding and holds several active projects under the cooperative agreement. Jordan presented and participated in panel discussions.

Jordan also attended the [EarthScope](https://www.earthscope.org/), [NHERI](https://www.designsafe-ci.org/facilities/experimental/), [OpenTopography](https://opentopography.org/), and [CTEMPs](https://ctemps.org/) Strategic Partnership Meeting in Seattle, WA March 9–11. The meeting brought together leaders from these four peer NSF-funded research infrastructure organizations to explore opportunities for deeper collaboration, particularly around near-surface geophysics, an area of overlap across all four groups.

## HydroShare

### 🔍HydroShare Discover Page Updates

HydroShare launched an updated Discover page to make it easier to find data and other resources! The new interface includes improved search across metadata (e.g., title, abstract, authors, keywords), autocomplete suggestions, and quick previews of resources directly in the results table.

![Screenshot of the updated HydroShare Discover page](/newsletter-images/2026-april/hydroshare-discover-page.jpg)

Explore the updated page at [https://www.hydroshare.org/discover](https://www.hydroshare.org/discover) and [follow this link](https://help.hydroshare.org/discovering-resources/discovering-data-and-models-from-hydroshare-users/) to learn more about the new discover page in the help documentation!

**HydroShare Resource Spotlight**: Each month we highlight a recently published HydroShare resource to share with the community!

This month’s HydroShare spotlight is a collection that contains the [National Ecological Observatory Network’s (NEON) Hydrologic Data Products Site Level Resources](https://hydroshare.org/resource/53718072f33646fa920f1b72d7b403eb/). This collection contains one resource for each NEON site. Each NEON site resource provides information on the hydrologic data products offered at the site. Users can find details on all hydrologic data products published from observational, instrumented, or remote sensing methods across air, land, and water. No NEON data are stored in this resource, but users can use direct links and code resources to be quickly directed to data products of interest. Developed to support CUAHSI’s [Introduction to NEON for Hydrology cyberseminar series](https://www.cuahsi.org/cyberseminars/series/introduction-to-neon-for-hydrology) last month, this collection enables NEON’s data to be discovered alongside other data in HydroShare.
